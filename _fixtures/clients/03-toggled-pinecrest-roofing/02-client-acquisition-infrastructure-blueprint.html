Infrastructure Overview

This is the path an enquiry takes from the moment it arrives to the moment the roofer is standing at the door, and the version below is the one we build for you. Every stage that can run without a person waiting on it does. The two that cannot are the ones where judgement or a licensed trade is the whole point.

The Conversion Path

01
Capture

Role. Get every enquiry into one system regardless of which channel it arrived on.

Current break point. Three separate routes into the business today, none of which share a queue: a phone line with no fallback, a form with nothing behind it, and a social page sitting on its own.

What we deploy. One tracked number with missed-call text-back, a webchat widget that continues in SMS, the social inbox connected, and the site form rewritten to feed the same queue.

Deployed by usKPI · Share of inbound enquiries that reach the system with a timestamp on them.

Speed to Lead

Role. Make sure the first reply is ours, on every channel, every time.

Current break point. A review describes an enquiry through the site that waited days, and nothing automated sits behind that form today.

What we deploy. Instant auto-reply on form and chat, missed-call text-back on the phone, and an after-hours response that covers the evenings and weekends your listing shows as closed.

Deployed by usKPI · Median first-response time, measured in minutes rather than hours.
04
Nurture

Role. Stay present between the quote and the decision, and between one job and the next.

Current break point. What reaches an unbooked quote is not visible from outside, and most local trades stop after one or two attempts.

What we deploy. A multi-touch email and SMS sequence on every unbooked quote, a longer branch for the not-yet leads, and a seasonal reactivation run at the past-customer list.

Deployed by usKPI · Share of quoted jobs that book within ninety days.
05
Book

Role. Let a ready customer hold a real slot without needing a phone conversation.

Current break point. Booking runs through phone and email only, so it can only happen while somebody in the office is free.

What we deploy. The booking page inside your GoHighLevel sub-account, wired to roofer availability, with a text-to-pay deposit link that turns a held slot into a committed one.

Deployed by usKPI · Share of bookings taken without a phone call.
06
Show-Up and Recovery

Role. Protect the booked slot and pick up the ones that fall over.

Current break point. Nothing between the booking and the visit currently reminds anybody, and the wait grows as booking moves online.

What we deploy. Confirmation and reminder messages on every booking, a same-day text before arrival, and a two-step recovery sequence on any appointment that does not happen.

Deployed by usKPI · Share of booked appointments attended.

Lead Qualification
ⓘlead qualification. Sorting incoming leads so you spend time on the ones most likely to become paying customers.
Framework

Priority Lead
90–100

An active leak, or a replacement-sized job, inside the service area.

Action. Page the on-call roofer and call back immediately.

Response. Under five minutes, day or night. · Owner. On-call roofer, with the office copied. · Method. Phone first, SMS as the fallback if the call is not picked up.

Qualified Lead
70–89

A real job in the service area with no immediate urgency behind it.

Action. Call back the same business day and put a quote in writing.

Response. Within one hour during business hours. · Owner. Office. · Method. Phone, then the standard quote follow-up sequence.

Nurture Lead
40–69

Interested but not ready, or planning work for a future season.

Action. Send the information they asked for and place them in the longer sequence.

Response. Same day, automated. · Owner. Automation. · Method. Email and SMS, spaced across the weeks the decision actually takes.

Low Fit Lead
0–39

Outside the service area, or asking about work you do not take on.

Action. Reply honestly, point them somewhere useful, and keep the record.

Response. Same day, automated. · Owner. Automation. · Method. A single courteous email; no sequence.

Intake & scoring questions
1. What is going on?

Separates an emergency from planned work before anybody picks up a phone. · an active leak scores highest; planned maintenance scores lowest.

2. How soon does this need doing?

Urgency is what decides the callback order. · Today adds the most; planning ahead routes to the longer sequence.

3. What is your postal code?

Tells us immediately whether the job is inside the service area. · Outside the service area caps the score into the low-fit band.

4. Is this a home or a commercial property?

Changes who calls back and how the quote is written. · Commercial and strata add a small amount for job size.

5. Best number to reach you on

The callback is a phone call, so the number has to be right. · No score; a missing number blocks the priority route.

Follow-Up Operating System

SMS follow-up framework
Send Day 1 — step 1 of the 60-day sequence on an unbooked quote

Hi [First name], it is Pinecrest. Just checking the quote reached you okay. Happy to talk it through, or you can pick a slot yourself here: [booking link]

Picks the conversation back up the day after, while it is still the thing they were thinking about. · On reply: Replies route to the office queue; a booking closes the sequence automatically.

Send Day 4 — step 3 of the 60-day sequence on an unbooked quote

Quick one [First name] — is it still doing the same thing, or has it got worse? Changes what I would suggest doing first.

One question that takes four words to answer, which is what restarts a thread that has gone quiet. · On reply: Any reply reopens the conversation with the office; the word URGENT pages the on-call number.

Send Day 11 — step 5 of the 60-day sequence on an unbooked quote

Hi [First name], is there one thing about the quote holding this up? Tell me which and I will answer it straight rather than guessing.

Names the real blocker instead of asking for a decision they have not made yet. · On reply: Replies go to the office; a booking or a reply stops the rest of the sequence.

Send Day 23 — step 7 of the 60-day sequence on an unbooked quote

Hi [First name], still on the list for this season, or has it moved to next year? Either is a fine answer — it just tells me when to check back.

A genuine permission check that also sorts the list into now and later. · On reply: Later parks the record until the seasonal run; done stops everything.

Send Day 38 — step 9 of the 60-day sequence on an unbooked quote

Hi [First name], we have space in the calendar over the next fortnight if you want it done before the spring thaw. Slots are here: [booking link]

Real availability stated plainly, with no invented deadline attached to it. · On reply: A booking closes the sequence; anything else leaves the last two steps to run.

Send Day 52 — step 11 of the 60-day sequence on an unbooked quote

Last one from me [First name] — do you want this booked in? One word back and I will either sort it or leave you to it.

A direct, warm ask at the point where anything less direct gets ignored. · On reply: Yes routes straight to the office to book; anything else lets the final email close it out.

Booking & Show-Up System

What to expect
  • You choose a window rather than being given a whole morning.
  • A confirmation text arrives within a minute with the roofer's name.
  • A reminder lands the day before with what to clear access to.
  • You get a message when the roofer leaves the previous job.
  • Nothing is charged beyond the callout fee without a written price you agreed.
Appointment positioning

The visit is a diagnosis, not a sales call. The roofer tells you what has failed, what it costs to fix, and whether they would fix it or replace it in your position. You decide after that, with the price in writing.

Show-up quality

The reminders exist to keep attendance where it is as booking volume moves online and the wait between booking and visit gets longer.

Reschedule framing

Rescheduling is offered as a one-word reply on every message, because a customer who cannot easily move an appointment tends to simply not be home for it.
